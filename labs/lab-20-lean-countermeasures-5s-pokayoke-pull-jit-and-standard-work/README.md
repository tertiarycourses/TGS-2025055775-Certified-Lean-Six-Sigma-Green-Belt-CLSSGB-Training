# Lab 20 — Lean Countermeasures — 5S, Poka-Yoke, Pull, JIT and Standard Work

**DMAIC phase:** IMPROVE  |  **Lab type:** Core  |  **Course:** Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (CLSSGB) Training (TGS-2025055775)

## Objective

Apply proven Lean countermeasures to the identified wastes (A5).

## Scenario

Northwind Retail Distribution Centre fulfils online orders from a 12,000 sqm warehouse. Customers are complaining about late deliveries and the company is receiving negative feedback on social media. The Distribution Manager has asked you, as Green Belt, to lead a DMAIC project on the order-fulfilment process — from order release to carrier handover.

## What you will build

A countermeasure plan applying 5S, poka-yoke, pull and standard work to your process.

**Tools and techniques:** 5S, poka-yoke levels, pull system, kanban, JIT, heijunka, jidoka, standard work

Open the workbook(s) below from this lab's `data/` folder. Every lab uses the same Northwind baseline month, so the figures reconcile across the whole course.

- **[`lab20-5s-waste-audit.xlsx`](data/lab20-5s-waste-audit.xlsx)** — A 5S audit of the four warehouse areas plus the waste walk observations tagged against the eight DOWNTIME wastes.

**Working with `lab20-5s-waste-audit.xlsx`:**

- For each observation, propose a countermeasure: 5S, Poka-Yoke, pull/JIT, or standard work.
- A Poka-Yoke makes the error impossible, obvious or difficult — which of these would stop a wrong-bin pick?

## Steps

### Step 1

Open lab20-5s-waste-audit.xlsx — 5S scores for each warehouse area plus the waste walk log. Score each area out of 25; any area under 15 needs a 5S event first.

### Step 2

Design a poka-yoke for your highest-severity defect. Work through the three levels: prevent the error occurring, detect it as it occurs, or detect it before it passes downstream.

### Step 3

Prefer prevention over detection — a connector that only fits one way beats a checklist asking the operator to check the orientation.

### Step 4

Evaluate whether a pull system would help: does work get pushed into the process faster than it can be consumed, creating queues?

### Step 5

Design a kanban signal that lets a downstream step pull work only when it has capacity.

### Step 6

Consider heijunka (load levelling) if demand arrives in peaks — levelling the load reduces both overtime and idle time.

### Step 7

Apply jidoka: build in the ability to stop the process automatically when a defect is detected, rather than continuing to produce defects.

### Step 8

Write standard work for the improved method: the sequence, the takt time, the standard WIP and the quality checks.

### Step 9

Make the standard work visual — a photo or one-page diagram at the workstation beats a document in a shared drive.

### Step 10

Map each countermeasure back to the specific waste from your DOWNTIME analysis in Lab 8.

## Check your work

Every countermeasure traces to a specific waste, and your poka-yoke prevents or detects the error rather than relying on someone remembering to check.
