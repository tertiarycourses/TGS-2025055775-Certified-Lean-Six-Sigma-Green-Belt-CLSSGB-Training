# Lab 21 — Solution Selection Matrix and Cost-Benefit Analysis

**DMAIC phase:** IMPROVE  |  **Lab type:** Core  |  **Course:** Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (CLSSGB) Training (TGS-2025055775)

## Objective

Select the most feasible and impactful solutions using weighted criteria (A3, A5).

## Scenario

Northwind Retail Distribution Centre fulfils online orders from a 12,000 sqm warehouse. Customers are complaining about late deliveries and the company is receiving negative feedback on social media. The Distribution Manager has asked you, as Green Belt, to lead a DMAIC project on the order-fulfilment process — from order release to carrier handover.

## What you will build

A weighted solution selection matrix with ranked results and a cost-benefit analysis.

**Tools and techniques:** Solution selection matrix, weighted criteria, feasibility, cost, impact, effort-impact grid, cost-benefit analysis

## Data for this lab

Open the workbook(s) below from this lab's `data/` folder. Every lab uses the same Northwind baseline month, so the figures reconcile across the whole course.

- **[`lab21-solution-selection-scoring.xlsx`](data/lab21-solution-selection-scoring.xlsx)** — The shortlisted ideas from Lab 19 with the weighted criteria to score against and the financial inputs for the cost-benefit analysis.

**Working with `lab21-solution-selection-scoring.xlsx`:**

- Criteria weights: Feasibility 25%, Cost 20%, Impact 40%, Time 15%. Score each 1-5, then weight.
- Weighted score = (Feas x 0.25) + (Cost x 0.20) + (Impact x 0.40) + (Time x 0.15).
- Then calculate payback period = Est. Cost / (Annual Benefit / 12) in months, and rank.
- Do the two rankings — weighted score and payback — agree? If not, which do you present and why?

## Steps

### Step 1

Open lab21-solution-selection-scoring.xlsx — the eight shortlisted solutions with their cost and annual benefit, and blank columns to score.

### Step 2

Define the selection criteria across the columns: Feasibility (is it practical to implement?), Cost (is it cost-effective?), Impact (does it significantly reduce the defect?), and Time to implement (how quickly?).

### Step 3

Assign a weight to each criterion reflecting what matters to the sponsor — impact and cost usually carry the highest weights.

### Step 4

Agree the scoring scale before scoring anything, and write down what a 1 and a 5 mean for each criterion so scores are comparable.

### Step 5

Score every solution against every criterion with the team, not alone — divergent scores usually reveal a hidden assumption worth discussing.

### Step 6

Calculate the weighted score for each solution: multiply each score by its criterion weight and sum across the row.

### Step 7

Rank the solutions by total weighted score and identify the top three.

### Step 8

Cross-check the ranking on an effort-impact grid — look for the high-impact, low-effort quick wins in the top-left quadrant.

### Step 9

Run the cost-benefit analysis from the workbook: payback period = Est. Cost / (Annual Benefit / 12) in months. Check whether the payback ranking agrees with your weighted-score ranking.

### Step 10

Sanity-check the selected solution against the root cause it addresses and confirm it does not simply move the problem downstream.

### Step 11

Document the rationale for the selection — a sponsor will ask why the obvious expensive option was not chosen.

## Check your work

Your matrix has weighted criteria with a defined scoring scale, every solution is scored, and your top-ranked solution has a payback period calculated.
